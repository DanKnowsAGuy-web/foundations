# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Inquirers and catechumens approaching the Eastern Orthodox Church: seekers
weighing a serious spiritual commitment, converts coming from other Christian
traditions or from no faith at all, and cradle-Orthodox who want to understand
what they have inherited. Many arrive disillusioned with the thin, casual
aesthetics of modern and low-church culture and are drawn precisely by the
promise of something ancient, weighty, and beautiful.

Their context: this is not a casual browse. They are contemplating something
that could reorder their life. They want to know what the class is, who teaches
it, what it asks of them, and whether the people behind it are serious. The page
meets someone at a threshold.

## Product Purpose

Foundations is a catechesis class for the Eastern Orthodox Church. The site
exists to invite inquirers into the class and to convey, before a single word is
read, that the faith it teaches is true, good, and beautiful. Its job is to make
the seriousness and beauty of Orthodoxy felt, lower the fear of stepping through
the door, and turn an interested visitor into someone who shows up.

Success: a visitor who came curious leaves resolved to attend, and feels the
page itself was an act of care rather than a marketing funnel.

## Brand Personality

Calm, refined, warm, human. Reverent without being austere; confident in the
tradition without lecturing. The voice is that of a learned, hospitable guide,
not a salesperson and not an academic. Three words: reverent, hospitable,
unhurried. The page should evoke the feeling of stepping from a noisy street
into a quiet church: stillness, light, and the sense that beauty here is not
ornament but argument.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS / startup landing pages: gradient blobs, cream backgrounds,
  identical icon-card grids, an eyebrow over every section, hero-metric stats.
- Corporate and stocky: stock smiling faces, navy-and-gray, soulless enterprise
  templates, anything that feels like a brochure.
- Loud and overdesigned: maximalism, stacked effects, visual noise, anything
  that performs rather than serves.
- Kitsch religious web: clip-art crosses, garish gold gradients, cluttered
  parish-bulletin layouts, low-resolution icon stock. Sacred imagery deserves
  the resolution and restraint it would receive in a museum.

## Design Principles

- **Beauty is the argument.** Beauty, goodness, and truth are the thesis, so the
  craft of the page must itself be beautiful. The design persuades before the
  copy does.
- **Reverence through restraint.** Negative space, stillness, and a few decisive
  elements over abundance. Silence is part of the liturgy; let the page breathe.
- **Classic Western craft meets Eastern Orthodoxy.** Draw on the discipline of
  classical typography and proportion to frame Orthodox imagery and color. Two
  traditions in conversation, neither flattened into the other.
- **Meet the seeker at the threshold.** Lower fear, answer the unspoken
  questions, extend hospitality. Invitation, never pressure.
- **Honor the sacred.** Icons, words, and symbols of the faith are treated with
  the dignity they hold, never as decoration or stock filler.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1, including any
text set over imagery or saturated liturgical color. Full keyboard navigation
and visible focus states. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` with calm crossfade or
instant alternatives. Readable line lengths (65–75ch) for any catechetical prose.
